FastDRS: Building an End-to-End Medical Computer Vision System in Python

Saturday, 10 October 2026 15:45 – 16:15 Albert Hall

Michael Mukiibi

Rift Research Labs

Abstract

Diabetic retinopathy is one of the leading causes of preventable vision loss, yet screening remains difficult to scale in many resource-constrained settings. FastDRS is a Python-based computer vision toolkit developed to explore how modern deep-learning models can support automated diabetic retinopathy screening while keeping practical deployment considerations in view.

In this talk, I will walk through the engineering journey behind FastDRS, from preparing retinal image data and establishing a baseline to experimenting with and evaluating multiple deep-learning architectures, including ResNet, DenseNet, ConvNeXt, Swin Transformer, and MobileNet. Rather than treating accuracy as the only objective, we examine metrics such as macro F1, sensitivity, specificity, inference latency, parameter count, and model size to understand the trade-offs involved in building a useful screening system.

The talk will also explore the Python ecosystem behind the project, including PyTorch-based experimentation, reproducible evaluation, experiment tracking, model comparison, and the path toward API and mobile deployment. I will discuss some of the challenges encountered during development, including class imbalance, model selection, evaluation beyond accuracy, and the tension between predictive performance and deployability.

Finally, I will share what I learned from taking FastDRS from an ML experiment toward a more complete software system, and discuss how Python can serve as the connective layer between research experimentation, model engineering, evaluation, and real-world AI deployment.

The goal is to present another medical AI model, but to show the engineering decisions involved in turning a computer vision idea into a system that can be tested, measured, packaged, and ultimately deployed.
